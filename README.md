# Mobber

*A gangland picture in three reels.*

A first-person shooter styled as a lost 1931 gangster film: black and white, film grain, projector flicker, silent-movie title cards, scenes that open and close with an iris, and a piano playing along.

Your brother Eddie kept the books at the Blue Note, and kept them honest. The Vitale family pulled him out of the river. Over one night you follow his ledger from the club to the docks to the Hotel Regent, walking the rainy streets between them, and the picture ends at dawn with a chase across the hotel roof: stop Don Vitale before his autogiro flies him out of town. When you have him on his knees, what happens to him is your choice, and the ending and the morning paper follow from it.

**Play it:** https://heidenreich.github.io/mobber/

## Controls

| | Desktop | Phone |
|---|---|---|
| Move | WASD (Shift to run) | Left thumb: up/down walks, left/right turns |
| Lean round a corner | Z (left) and C (right) | — |
| Aim | Mouse | Drag with your right thumb, including on FIRE |
| Shoot | Click or Space | Hold FIRE. It also fires on its own when a gangster is under the crosshair |
| Reload | R (automatic when empty) | Automatic |
| Swap guns | Q, 1/2/3 or the mouse wheel | SWAP |
| Search for secret walls | Walk into a wall, or press E | Walk into a wall |
| Crack a safe | Stand at it and hold E | Stand still at it |
| Pause | Esc | — |
| Music on or off | M | the gear, top right |
| Settings | the gear, top right (Esc first to free the mouse) | the gear, top right |

The gear opens the projection booth: music, sound effects, look speed, difficulty (Sunday stroll, Regular, Hard-boiled), a buzz when you're hit on a phone, and starting the picture over. Your choices and the scene you last reached are remembered in the browser, so closing the tab doesn't send you back to the start.

Doors open by themselves as you walk up to them. Some walls hide rooms: look for faint scrape marks near the floor.

Ammo is limited, and so is what you can carry. You start with a loaded revolver and 12 spare rounds. A few boxes of rounds are lying around, and gangsters you put down leave something behind: sometimes a few revolver rounds, sometimes their tommy gun, sometimes cash or something the fence will buy. The first heavy you put down gives up his sawn-off shotgun, which settles things up close and does little at a distance. With both guns empty, a click swings the gun at anyone in arm's reach. Your health carries over from scene to scene, and first-aid kits are rare.

Gangsters who hear gunfire come looking for you, finding their way around walls and through doors. The first to see you shouts for the others near him. Between bursts a goon may duck out of your line of fire, a hit may knock his hat off, and the last hired gun left standing loses his nerve and runs.

Their bullets take time to reach you, and you can see them coming: a bright streak in the air. Step sideways and it goes past; stand still and it finds you. Some gangsters aim ahead of a moving target, so walking in a straight line isn't always enough, and changing direction is. Walls, closed doors and tall cover stop bullets.

Z and C lean your head and gun out to one side while your feet stay where they are. From behind a corner or a doorway that's enough to see and shoot, with only your head for them to aim at, and letting go puts the wall back between you and whatever is already on its way. You can't run while leaning.

Not all of them carry tommy guns. A knife man in a flat cap has no gun at all, but he runs faster than you walk: he raises the blade before he strikes, and a step back makes it miss. On the streets a rifleman may be waiting at an upstairs window; the lamplight catches his scope a moment before a fast, well-aimed shot, and he can be shot out of his window. The bagman, carrying the night's takings, doesn't fight: he runs for the alarm bell, and if he reaches it every gun in the place knows where you are. Drop him first and the bag is yours.

Until something alerts him, a gangster only sees what's in front of him, and now and then he looks behind. Nobody is watching the spot where you come into a scene, and nobody can spot you while the picture opens, so you always get a moment to look around. If you can see his back, he can't see you: keep out of the lamplight, don't get too close, and you can slip past. A shot at a man who never saw it coming does three times the damage.

Not everyone is in the Vitale outfit. The Blue Note has patrons at its tables, a bartender who'll tell you where the office is, and a man at the piano: his playing is the music in there, and it stops on a fistful of wrong notes when the first shot is fired. The patrons scatter, some for whichever way out is furthest from the gunfire and some for the nearest place to hide, and the staff get their heads down. There are people in the rest of town too: someone out in the rain on Mulberry Street, working men on the Waterfront and at Pier 9, passers-by and the Regent's doorman on the square (he has something to say), and guests on the hotel's top floor. A bullet that finds a bystander is counted against you in the morning paper.

Cash is worth picking up, because the fence is where most of your supplies come from. First-aid kits and tommy gun drums are scarce on purpose, gangsters don't always have rounds on them, and your health carries from scene to scene as it is (never below 30 on Regular, 15 on Hard-boiled, 50 on Sunday stroll, which also patches you up for the finale). If you're shot down, the retake starts with at least 60 health and 24 revolver rounds on Regular (75 and 30 on Sunday stroll, 40 and 18 on Hard-boiled), and each further death in the same scene adds 10 health and 6 rounds, up to full health and full pockets, so a scene you reached nearly empty-handed can still be got through. The last fence is on Regent Square: after it come the lift, the top floor and the roof, so what you buy there has to last. On each street a fence deals through a walk-up window at the end of an alley: look for a pawnbroker's three balls and a LOANS sign on the wall by the alley's mouth, and the lit window down it. Walk up and press E (on a phone his list comes up by itself). He sells revolver rounds, tommy gun drums, shells, a doctor who puts you back to full health, and a steel vest that takes half of every hit until it has taken 60 points. Click or tap a line to buy it, or press its number. A shot nearby brings his shutter down, and it stays down for a few seconds.

He buys as well. Gangsters sometimes have a pocket watch, cufflinks, a ring or a cigarette case on them, and each of the Don's doubles wears a gold watch. Pick them up (the status bar shows how many you're carrying next to your cash) and the first line of his list sells the lot.

Each chapter has something besides shooting. The ledger at the Blue Note is in the office safe, and partway through working the dial the whole club hears you. The lookouts on the Waterfront can be slipped past. Regent Square has a fountain in the middle and trees round it, all of them cover, and you'll want it: the Vitale men fall back to hold the hotel door, and there's a rifle in a window beside it. In the Pier 9 warehouse, crates hang in slings from the crane: a shot that reaches one before it reaches anybody parts the rope, and the load comes down on whoever is under it. That can be several gangsters at once, a working man who had nothing to do with it, or you. Fire a shot there and the men at the pier door hear it, and get into their places to hold it. At the Hotel Regent you ride the lift, and Vitale men are waiting at every landing on the way up. No two landings are alike: one is as you'd expect, one has its lights out and a knife somewhere in the dark, one has tables tipped over with men shooting from behind them, and on the last a man with a shotgun is standing right outside the gate.

The roof is a chase in three stretches, marked off by rows of chimney stacks: the stairhead, the water towers, the landing pad. The men posted on each stretch wait for you to reach it. The Don runs each one and doesn't shoot while he runs, and a hit on a running man only grazes him. At the end of the first two he turns and fights; get close, or hurt him enough, and he runs again. At the pad he has to wait for the autogiro's rotor to come up to speed, and the gauge at the top of the screen shows how long you have. The sky lightens as the chase goes on. There's a first-aid kit and a tommy gun drum by the stairhead.

Each chapter has one of Vitale's lieutenants: Frankie Lace, who runs the Blue Note; Big Otto, foreman of Pier 9; and Mr. Finch, the Don's own man, on the hotel's top floor. Each has something to say when he sees you, takes a good deal more putting down than the rest, and shows his name and how much fight he has left while you're at it. Each leaves a bankroll and something for the fence.

A shot that misses can still hit something. Lamps can be shot out, and gangsters see less far when you stand in the dark. The dark stops hiding you when you shoot: the flash of your gun shows them where you are for a moment. The bottles behind a bar and shop windows shatter. Crates, cars, water towers and other solid props are cover: they block bullets and sight both ways.

## Running it locally

There's no build step. The game is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript modules. Browsers won't load modules from a double-clicked file, so serve the folder:

```sh
npm start            # then open http://127.0.0.1:8000/
```

`npm start` needs only Node. Any static server works too, for example `python3 -m http.server`.

Working on the game? [DEVELOPMENT.md](DEVELOPMENT.md) covers the tests, how the code is organized, and where to make common changes.
